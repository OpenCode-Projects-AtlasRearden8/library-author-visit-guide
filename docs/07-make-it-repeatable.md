# Make It Repeatable

One visit costs a month of nervous energy. The second should cost ten
minutes and a checklist. That difference is not talent; it is a kit.
Everything the last six chapters told you to decide, print, or pack
belongs in one box and four folders, so visit number two starts from a
kit instead of from scratch. This chapter is the inventory.

## The shape of the kit

One physical box in the closet of your car, four named folders on your
computer. Rule one: **everything lives in the kit or it does not
exist.** The night before a visit, you grab a box, not a memory.

Physical box, packed once, restocked after every visit:

- laptop charger and every video adapter, arrive-in-the-bag level of
  certainty;
- USB stick with the demo backup slides and screenshots, checked
  monthly;
- printed spares: one read-aloud copy in 14-point-plus, six session
  schedules, ten bookmarks, three handouts;
- the table cloth, the small signage set, card reader, cash box with
  change at rest levels;
- tape, marker, scissors, breath mints, and the water bottle that
  travels.

Folders on the computer (the
[repeatable kit inventory](../templates/repeatable-kit-inventory.md)
is the full printable list):

1. **Pitch kit** -- the letter, the venue field notes, the calendar
   log of who was asked and what happened.
2. **Session kit** -- the fifty-minute plan, the read-aloud passage
   (marked-up version and clean copy), the demo script plus backup
   screenshots, the slides if any.
3. **Sales kit** -- pricing card, ledger sheet, stock card, the
   receipt and tax note from your state's answer.
4. **Follow-up kit** -- thank-you letters, recap template, the
   newsletter signup sheet, the visit file per venue.

## The visit file: one venue, one page

Every venue gets a single page (a plain text or one-page sheet):

- contact person and the way they like to be reached;
- the dates pitched, the answers, the yes or no;
- room facts from the day: outlets, table, light, quirk ("the
  projector cuts out above row three");
- what worked, what to do differently, the numbers that travel: head
  count, copies sold, signups;
- one kind memory, because six months later that line hires you back.

Five rows per venue. That page is the actual asset this manual builds;
everything else is supplies.

## The session improves in versions, not in inspiration

- Keep the fifty-minute plan as **living version**: after each visit,
  adjust one segment and mark the change in the margin. One change per
  visit, no rewriting the spine.
- The read-aloud passage rotates seasonally, so returning guests hear
  something new; the old passage retires to the archive folder.
- The demo script gains a line every time something goes wrong: "Wi-Fi
  down at [Branch] on March the 4th -- switched to screenshots at
  minute 18 and nobody noticed."

## Ask the room three questions, then log them

At clean-up, ask two or three guests what worked. Ask the staff person
one question too: "Was there anything about the evening that made
your job easier or harder?" Their answer is programming-grade market
research, and it goes in the visit file word for word.

## The season calendar

Libraries plan quarterly, stores seasonally. Once your kit exists,
book ahead by pattern:

- one pitch wave every season to three to five venues;
- one repeat-date request every year for venues that had you;
- the quiet months belong to the biggest venues: pitch in January for
  National Library Week in April, and pitch bookstores six weeks
  before your book's birthday or the gift season.

A logger's note beats a resolution: the season after your first
visit, when a coordinator says, "Can you do the second Tuesday in
October?", the answer is a peek at your kit and a confident yes.

## The ten-minute pre-visit ritual

With the kit built, every future visit starts the same way: pull the
box, glance at the venue page, print the day-of checklist, update
three fields in the pitch letter, done. The only price of entry to
this calm is one afternoon, once, to build the kit. Build it this
week; future-you is already saying thank you.
