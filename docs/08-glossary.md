# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of every word this manual uses that a
calendar-coordinator might say and a first-time author might never
have. Written so you can read it on the drive over, once, and be done.

## Programming / adult services

The library department or librarian who fills the events calendar.
"Adult programming" does not mean anything spicy -- it means events
for grown-ups, as opposed to storytime for toddlers or teen clubs.
Address your pitch to this phrase and it reaches the right desk.

## Consignment

Selling at a bookstore by leaving your copies there until they sell.
The store keeps a share (often around 30-40%), pays you for the rest,
and can return unsold copies. Opposite of wholesale, where the store
buys the books upfront.

## Wholesale / royalty terms

Wholesale is the store buying copies from a distributor at a discount
before your event. "Royalty terms" is what little arrives in your
pocket per copy through that path. For local events, either is fine;
the point of chapter 5 is only to ask in advance which rail applies.

## A/V

Audio/visual: projector, screen, microphone, speakers, and the tangle
of cables that joins them. In small venues, treat all of it as
unproven until you have stood in the room and played something, as
described in the [A/V chapter](04-av-and-tables.md).

## Adapter

The small plug that joins a modern laptop to a venue's older port
(USB-C to HDMI and friends). Bring all of them. There is no adapter
you own that a venue cannot surprise you by lacking.

## Airplane-mode demo

Preparing your tool demonstration so it works with no network at all:
pre-saved screenshots, a locally-running model, or printed output.
Named after the switch you flip when a venue's Wi-Fi dies mid-sentence.

## Cast / screen mirroring

Sending your screen to a TV or projector wirelessly instead of a
cable. Works beautifully in meeting rooms about half the time. The
other half is why the airplane-mode demo exists.

## Reader / card reader

The little device or phone app that takes card payments (a reader or
an app without hardware). Test it at home with a 10-cent sale before
event night.

## Cash float

The supply of small bills and coins in your cash box so the first
"do you have change for a twenty" does not turn into a sprint to a
register. Ten ones and two fives is a starter float.

## Visit file

Your single page of facts about one venue: contact, room, dates,
numbers, one kind memory. Chapter 7 explains it. It is the asset the
whole kit exists to grow.

## Kit

The physical box plus four folders (pitch, session, sales,
follow-up) that make visit number two cost ten minutes of prep
instead of a month of nerves.

## Signup sheet

The clipboard at the book table where guests write their email for
your free newsletter if they want. Paper first, QR code as a backup;
voluntary by nature -- the sibling author-newsletter-handbook covers
what to send them after.

## Planted question

A guest (often a friend) you have asked beforehand to open the Q&A if
silence stretches. Old-fashioned and unfailingly effective, because
the first question is the hard one.

## Access needs

What a guest needs in order to fully take part: seating, a printed
handout, a loop for hearing aids, a room bright enough to read faces.
You never interrogate anyone about it; you say "access needs welcome,
just ask" and then quietly meet what gets asked.

## Large print

The umbrella rule of chapter 3: 18-point-plus for anything projected
or posted, 14-point-plus (better 16-18) for any handout, high
contrast, short lines.

## Plain English

Writing and speaking so a tired nurse after a double shift gets it on
the first pass: short sentences, jargon defined on first mention,
numbers in coffee-price units, and zero condescension.

## Fifty-minute session

The shape of the visit from
[Plan the Fifty Minutes](02-plan-the-fifty-minutes.md): welcome,
reading, five-minute story, low-key tool demo, first questions, open
Q&A, close and table time. The spine everything else hangs on.

## Day-of checklist

The one-page, tick-the-box list from
[templates](../templates/day-of-checklist.md) covering the week
before, the thirty minutes before guests, the session itself, and
clean-up. Print it; tape it to the box lid.
