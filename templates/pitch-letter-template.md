# Pitch Letter Template

The one-email pitch that earns a yes. Short, staff-oriented, and free
of pleading. Replace every bracketed slot with your real details --
and never send anything with brackets still in it. Chapter 1 explains
the reasoning; the follow-up ladder and timing live there too.

---

**To:** [events coordinator's email]
**From:** [Your name, your author email]
**Subject:** Free author visit for [VENUE] -- [MONTH] availability ([THREE QUICK FACTS])

Hi [first name, spelled correctly, from the venue's website],

My name is [YOUR NAME] and I am a local author of [TITLE OF BOOK] --
[one true line about the book: what it is and who it helps, in plain
English, e.g. "a plain-English guide to using everyday AI tools for
people who don't want the jargon"].

I'd like to offer [VENUE] a free author visit on [TWO preferred dates,
or a month and flexibility, e.g.]:

- **A free [fifty-minute] session** for [who it serves: local writers,
  retirees, small-business owners, parents, anyone curious about the
  topic but tired of the hype]. A short reading, questions, and a
  friendly low-key demonstration of [THE TOOLS BEHIND THE BOOK].
- **No cost and no work for your staff.** I bring everything -- my own
  laptop and adapters, an extension cord, signage, and my own table
  kit. I arrive [30] minutes early to set up and stay after to help
  reset the room.
- **[The venue's own benefit, one line, chosen for this venue:**
  for libraries -- a free event that draws attendance at a quiet time
  of year; for bookstores -- an author who drives foot traffic and
  will point guests at your register; for community centers -- an
  hour that fits your existing programming with no membership needed**.]**
- **Access needs welcome.** Large-print handouts are provided, I
  repeat every question aloud, and I keep the room bright enough for
  faces and print.

A little about me in two sentences: [second line of an ordinary life:
e.g. "I was a registered nurse before I started writing" plus one
credibility marker]. [OPTIONAL: one sentence: who has hosted you
before, or the number of copies sold to date if real and modest.]

Would [DATE 1] or [DATE 2] work for your calendar? Happy to answer
anything by email or phone -- [PHONE NUMBER], or just reply here.

Warmly,
[YOUR NAME]
[Book/site, one line only -- e.g. "The Massive AI Manual -- explained
like a friend would"]

---

## Fill-in notes

- **Subject line** carries the venue name, the word *free*, and your
  availability window. Coordinators scan subject lines to file pitches;
  make the filing easy.
- **The book sentence is the one place to sell.** One line, benefit
  first. If it takes two clauses to say what the book is for, cut
  until one clause holds.
- **The tools line stays humble.** "Friendly low-key demonstration"
  beats any word that sounds like a webinar. If your book is not a
  tools book, swap that bullet for a second audience-shaped promise.
- **Access bullet travels with you in every pitch.** It is both kind
  and, practically, a differentiator -- very few local pitches say it.
- **One preferred date is better than no date; two is better than
  one;** a whole flexible month is fine for libraries, worse for
  store calendars with a physical event wall.
- **Attach nothing.** A one-page poster or sell-sheet goes out when
  they ask for it, or on day 7 with your follow-up.
